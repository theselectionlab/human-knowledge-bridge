    What is the Moon made of?

    The Moon is made mostly of rock. It has a dusty, rocky surface, a crust, a deep mantle, and a small metallic core.

    Like Earth, it has three main layers: a crust, a mantle, and a core.

    Not cheese — Moon rock!

    Apollo astronauts brought home real Moon rocks. They are rock and dust, not dairy.

    How do we know?

    Moon rocks brought home

    Apollo astronauts collected 382 kg (842 lb) of Moon rock and soil and brought it to Earth.

    Direct samples

    Vibrations in the lunar ground

    Seismometers recorded moonquakes and meteorite impacts. Scientists used those recordings to infer the Moon’s interior.

    Measurements → inference

    The surface is sampled directly. The deep interior is inferred from measurements — no one has opened the Moon to look inside.

    An open question: the Moon’s crust is thicker on the far side than on the near side. Scientists are still working out why.

    Screen 3 · The Science Detective

    A Science Detective on the Moon

    Real hardware · Apollo 11 · 1969

    Passive Seismic Experiment (PSE)

    Seismometer: an instrument that measures ground movement.

    In 1969, astronauts left this instrument on the Moon. It listened for tiny vibrations in the lunar ground. Those vibrations gave scientists clues about what happens beneath the Moon’s surface.

    • First seismometer on the Moon
    • 4 sensors
    • Solar-powered
    • 3 weeks of data

    What it measured

    Vibrations from moonquakes and meteorite impacts.

    What it did not do

    • ✗ See inside the Moon directly.
    • ✗ Work through the lunar night.
    • ✗ Belong to the later Apollo network.

    Later missions

    More advanced seismometers were deployed at Apollo 12, 14, 15, and 16. They sent data until September 1977.

    You cannot see through the Moon. But vibrations can help scientists investigate what is hidden inside.

    Why this matters: This is one of the pieces of hardware NASA left on the Moon. Its measurements gave scientists the first detailed look at the Moon’s interior.

    Apollo 11’s instrument is not the same as the later Apollo seismometers.
