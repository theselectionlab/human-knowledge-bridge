    How we label claims

    Measured evidence
    Someone measured or recorded this directly.
    Scientific conclusion
    Scientists put several kinds of evidence together to figure this out.
    Scientific model
    Scientists build and test an explanation. No one has directly observed the whole thing.
    Open question
    Scientists don’t have enough evidence yet.

    The labels describe how we know, not how important the claim is. A model is not a guess. It is a tool scientists build, test, and revise.

    What this demo does not do

    • It does not answer every question. It has one checked Moon investigation.
    • It does not search the internet, retrieve live data, or generate new scientific answers.
    • It does not use an AI model at runtime. There is no large language model, no chatbot, and no API key.
    • It does not collect personal information, use cookies, or track anyone. Your question stays in this browser tab.
    • It does not claim to be a validated educational programme or an official NASA product.
    • It does not show the Moon’s interior to scale; the illustrations are simplified.
    • It does not cover the later Apollo missions in detail; it only notes that they were different.
    • It does not resolve scientific debates. It labels them as open questions.

    AI tools

    No AI model runs in this demo. AI tools were used in planning, drafting, coding, and testing; see AI_USAGE.md. Every scientific claim was checked by a human against the cited source.

    Privacy

    No accounts, no cookies, no analytics, no personal information. Your question stays in this browser tab and is not sent to a server.

    Accessibility

    Target: WCAG 2.2 AA. Keyboard, screen reader, 200% zoom, reduced motion, and contrast were tested. Known limitations are listed in the project README.
